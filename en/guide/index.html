01Getting started

The first time you open Luneki, it asks what you mostly edit (Landscape, Night & Astro / Portraits & Selfies / Both) and puts your most-used tools first. Every feature is always available, and you can change this later in Settings → Tool Order.

  1. Tap Choose Photo to pick from your library, or Import RAW to open a camera RAW file directly.
  2. You can also tap the camera icon to shoot inside the app and start editing right away.
  3. Added photos appear in the thumbnail strip at the bottom; tap to switch. Edited photos are marked, and switching back doesn't recompute anything.
Tip

The originals in your library are never modified, and removing a photo inside the app doesn't affect your library.

02The editing screen

The photo is at the top; the tool palette and the controls for the current tool are below.

  1. The tool palette is grouped by category: Presets, Basic, One-Tap, Landscape, Filter, Portrait, Body. Tap a category tab to jump to it.
  2. Tap any tool to add it as an adjustment; its sliders appear below.
  3. Press and hold the photo, or tap the eye icon at the top right, to compare with the original.
  4. Pinch to zoom in and inspect details; while zoomed, the visible area is re-rendered at full resolution. Tap Fit to return to the whole photo.
  5. The full-screen button at the top right hides all panels so you can see just the photo.
  6. Undo and Redo are in the toolbar; Copy and Paste apply a whole edit to another photo.
Tip

Double-tap a two-way slider (the kind with 0 in the middle) to reset it to zero.

03Adjustments & non-destructive editing

Every edit in Luneki is a separate adjustment, stacked on the photo like a layer.

  1. Tap Adjustments at the top to see every adjustment you've added.
  2. Open any adjustment again to change it, disable it (to compare) or delete it.
  3. You can add the same tool more than once — for example, two Selective adjustments for the sky and the ground.
Tip

Because the original is never altered, you can experiment freely and come back anytime.

04Astro & night photos

The most common problems with night and astro photos are noise, light pollution and flat contrast. A good order:

  1. AI Denoise: remove high-ISO noise first. Too much softens detail; 40–70 works well for stars.
  2. Shadow Detail: use Dehaze to cut through light pollution and haze, Clarity to bring out the Milky Way's structure, and Shadows / Highlights to recover the ends of the range.
  3. Basic: add some contrast and cool the temperature to bring the night sky back to deep blue.
  4. Lens: add Starburst to turn city lights into 4-, 6- or 8-point stars; use Vignette to draw the eye to the center.
Tip

For daytime haze, AI Dehaze works best; the dehaze in Shadow Detail is better suited to night and astro shots.

When smoke covers your fireworks, use Remove Smoke in Shadow Detail: broad smoke is subtracted and sparks are kept. A fully opaque clump of smoke can't be recovered — it only gets darker; for even, all-over haze use Dehaze instead.

05Multi-photo stacking (Fireworks, Starry, HDR)

Combine several photos into one to reduce noise, capture firework trails or extend dynamic range.

  1. Tap Layers at the top, then add photos — several shots of the same scene.
  2. Choose a mode in Stack mode: Fireworks (keeps the brightest parts of every shot), Starry (averages to reduce noise) or HDR (blends exposures and removes ghosting).
  3. Tap Align: Starry mode aligns the stars, other modes align the frame. Tap Revert Align to go back to unaligned.
  4. Each layer can have a mask — for example, Auto Sky stacks only the sky and keeps the landscape sharp.
  5. When you're done, choose Composite to keep editing the result with every tool.
Tip

A tripod gives the best results. For stars, use 4 or more shots; the shorter the gap between them, the less the stars trail.

Use Reopen Last Stack to continue editing later.

If a stacked fireworks shot is smoky, add Shadow Detail → Remove Smoke on the result to clear it. Shoot from a tripod at the same spot.

06Sky tools

The Sky tool detects the sky first and affects only the sky.

  1. Choose a detection mode: Auto (depth-based, looser) or Precise (semantic segmentation that hugs buildings and trees).
  2. Apply effects with the sliders: Blue Sky, Sunset (violet-pink gradient), Fire Sky (orange-red), Add Clouds, Depth (3D clouds), Clarity — or shift the sky's Hue directly.
  3. Turn on Show mask to see the detected area, and use the brush to add or erase and fix the edges.
Tip

Set Edge to Smooth to cover tiny gaps between leaves.

07Selective adjustments & masks

Want to brighten the subject and darken the sky? Build a mask with Selective, then adjust exposure, contrast, temperature and more.

  1. Under Region, choose Sky, Subject, Person or Background, or tap any object in the photo to select it (you can tap several).
  2. Switch to Brush to add areas by hand or remove them with Erase; the brush size is adjustable.
  3. Turn on Show mask to check the area, then adjust the sliders below.
Tip

Add several Selective adjustments to give the sky, the ground and people their own settings.

08Curves, HSL & filters

When you need precise color control:

  1. Curves: adjust the overall curve or the red, green and blue channels individually; open the full-screen editor for finer control points.
  2. HSL: adjust the hue, saturation and luminance of eight colors separately — for example, make only the sky bluer or skin tones clearer.
  3. Filter: 16 styles with adjustable Intensity that stack on top of your other adjustments.

09Portraits: retouching, skin & face shape

Portrait tools detect faces and bodies automatically. In group photos, choose who to adjust first.

  1. Deep Retouch: AI removes blemishes while keeping pores and texture — the best place to start.
  2. Skin Beauty: Auto Blemish, Wrinkle, Dark Circles, Whiten, Teeth Whiten, Skin Warmth and Skin Depth.
  3. Blemish: tap a spot or blemish to remove it; use Undo Last or Clear All.
  4. Reshape: Cheeks, Eye Size, Nose Size, Chin, Forehead, Face Width, Mouth, Double Chin and more, following real contours.
  5. Body: Body, Belly, Leg Length, Thighs, Arms, Shoulders, Hips and more; body parts outside the frame are never distorted.
Tip

Register people you photograph often (PRO): new photos recognize them automatically and ask whether to apply their personal settings.

10Makeup & hair color

The makeup tool covers 22 areas, each with an intensity slider and color swatches.

  1. Fastest: pick a look from Presets, such as Light Makeup, Glam Makeup, K-Glow or J-Sweet.
  2. Then open Makeup to fine-tune each area: Foundation, Contour, Nose Shadow, Highlight, Eyebrow, Eyeshadow, Glitter, Eyeliner, Mascara, Under-eye, Blush, Lipstick, Lip Gloss, Dewy Skin and more.
  3. Overall Intensity adjusts every area at once.
  4. Hair Color detects the hair and keeps the light and shadow of each strand — just pick a swatch.

11Background blur & bokeh

Simulate the shallow depth of field of a wide-aperture lens.

  1. Add Background Blur; the app finds people automatically and keeps them sharp. You can also tap or paint the people you want to keep sharp.
  2. Aperture controls the amount of blur; Focus and Edge adjust the sharp area and the transition.
  3. Turn on Bokeh and background lights become round bokeh balls; adjust Bokeh Size, Bokeh Softness, Aperture Shape and the Cat-Eye effect near the edges.
Tip

Night backgrounds with lights give the most beautiful bokeh.

12Draw

Draw or write freely on the photo. Your drawing is its own adjustment, so you can disable or delete it anytime.

  1. Add Draw from the Filter category.
  2. Pick a color (10 swatches, or the color wheel at the end for any color), then set Size and Opacity.
  3. Draw on the photo with your finger or Apple Pencil; switch to Erase to remove only what you drew — the photo itself is never erased.
  4. Undo Stroke removes strokes one at a time; Clear All starts over.
Tip

Strokes are redrawn at the original resolution, so lines stay crisp in full-resolution exports.

Drawing sits on top of every other adjustment, so later face reshaping or color changes won't distort it.

13Erase Object

Remove passers-by, clutter, power lines or anything you don't want.

  1. Add Erase Object and brush over what you want to remove.
  2. Lift your finger and AI fills in the background automatically.
  3. For small spots, a single tap with Blemish is faster.
Tip

Paint slightly beyond the object's edges for a more natural fill.

14Presets & batch

Save the adjustments you love and apply them in one tap next time.

  1. Built-in presets are grouped into Portrait, Makeup and Scene (Night, Golden Hour, Sunny, Starry and more).
  2. After editing a photo, use Save Preset in Presets (up to 3 on the free version, unlimited with PRO).
  3. In the thumbnail strip, tap Manage → select several photos to paste an edit onto all of them or export the selection (batch export is PRO).

15Export

Tap Export at the top right, choose your options, and save to your library.

  1. Format: JPEG, HEIC, PNG or TIFF, with optional 16-bit Deep Color and a Quality setting.
  2. Size: Original or 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 4K (the free version goes up to 1080p).
  3. Optionally turn on Keep EXIF / ICC to keep EXIF and the color profile.
  4. The watermark's position and size are adjustable; PRO can turn it off.
Tip

During batch export, stay on the screen and keep it unlocked until everything is finished.

16iPad & Apple Pencil

On iPad, Luneki switches to a split layout: a large canvas on the left, tools and sliders on the right.

  1. Landscape, and portrait on the 13-inch iPad, switch to the split layout automatically; Split View and narrower windows keep the standard layout.
  2. After you draw with Apple Pencil once, brushes and Draw respond only to Pencil, and fingers pan and zoom — no accidental strokes.
  3. With a hover-capable Apple Pencil, a circle shows the brush area before you touch the screen.
  4. Draw varies the width with Pencil pressure. You can turn this behavior off in Settings → Draw with Apple Pencil only.
